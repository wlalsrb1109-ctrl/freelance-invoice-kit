How to write an invoice a client pays without a reminder

Most late payments are not a cash-flow problem — they are a paperwork problem. An invoice that is unambiguous about who is billing whom, for what, and by when gets approved faster, because the person paying it does not have to email you to ask.

  1. Number it. A unique, sequential number (INV-2026-014) is what your client's accounts team files and what your tax office expects. The generator starts one for you and keeps counting.
  2. Say exactly what the money buys. “Design work — 12 hours” is weaker than “Homepage redesign — wireframes, 3 concepts, 2 revision rounds (12 h)”. Vague lines get questioned; specific lines get approved.
  3. Show the tax and the total separately. Subtotal, discount, tax, total. If reverse charge applies, print the note instead of inventing a zero-rated line.
  4. Put a date on it. “Net 14” is a real term; “whenever” is not. The due date is printed on the page, so a polite reminder writes itself.
  5. Tell them how to pay. Bank transfer with IBAN, Wise, PayPal — the details on the invoice are the difference between payment today and a reply asking for them.

Everything you type stays in this browser. There is no account, no upload and no server that could lose your client list — which is also why the invoice renders instantly as you type.

What every invoice needs

Requirements vary by country, but almost every tax authority asks for the same core set. The fields in this generator map to them one-to-one:

  • The word “Invoice” and a unique invoice number
  • Issue date and payment due date
  • Your business name, address and tax/VAT ID
  • The client's name, address and, for B2B, their tax/VAT ID
  • A description of each service, the quantity and the unit rate
  • Subtotal, any discount, the tax rate and amount, and the total due
  • Currency, and the payment details

For EU cross-border B2B work, add both VAT numbers and the reverse-charge note. For US sales tax, use the “Sales tax” label. This page is a tool, not tax advice — check your local rules.

Charging the right rate

An invoice is only as good as the number on it. Work backwards from the take-home pay you actually want, add your expenses, taxes and a buffer for slow months, and divide by the hours you can truly bill.

Frequently asked questions

Is this invoice generator really free?

Yes. Every feature on this page — logo upload, tax and discount, multi-currency, unlimited invoices and the PDF — is free, with no account and no watermark on your invoice. The site is supported by advertising and by an optional paid template bundle.

Where is my invoice data stored?

Only in your own browser, in local storage on this device. The invoice is built entirely in your browser, so your client names, rates and bank details are never uploaded to a server. Clearing your browser data deletes the draft, so save the PDF when you are done.

How do I get a PDF instead of a printed page?

Press Print / Save PDF: your browser's print dialog opens with everything except the invoice sheet hidden. Choose “Save as PDF” as the destination. Margins are preset, so the invoice lands on one A4 or US Letter page.

Is a PDF invoice valid for tax purposes?

In most countries an invoice PDF needs the same details as a paper one: a unique invoice number, the date, both parties' names and addresses, a description of the work, the amount, tax and total. Rules differ by jurisdiction (for example EU VAT numbers and reverse charge), so check your local requirements — this tool gives you the fields, not tax advice.

Can I use it for quotes and receipts too?

Yes. Switch the document type to Quote, Receipt, Credit note or Pro forma and the heading, numbering and totals adjust. One tool covers the whole client cycle.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. The form is a single column on mobile and the preview scales to fit. Printing from iOS or Android gives you “Save as PDF” in the share sheet.
